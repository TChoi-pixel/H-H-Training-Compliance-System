Self-assurance cycle

The operating model used throughout this system.
Obligation → Risk → Control → Evidence → Monitoring → Finding → Corrective Action → Effectiveness Review → Continuous Improvement
A control is not treated as effective merely because a policy exists. Assurance is based on whether the control operates in practice and whether evidence supports the intended outcome.

Risk & controls register

Links material RTO compliance risks to preventive/detective controls, accountable owners, control effectiveness and residual risk.

Risk methodology: inherent risk = likelihood (1–5) × consequence (1–5), banded Low / Medium / High / Critical. Residual risk applies an internal control-effectiveness adjustment — Effective moves the band down two levels, Partially Effective one level, Ineffective leaves it unchanged. This is an internal prioritisation tool, not an ASQA-prescribed scoring model.

Risk-based internal assurance program

Schedules assurance work according to material risk, regulatory change, complaints, validation findings, delivery changes and other triggers — not simply a fixed annual checklist.

Assessment & validation assurance

Risk-based monitoring of assessment quality and validation. Internal planned dates can be earlier than the regulatory maximum. Every training product must be validated at least once every five years, with frequency and sample size adjusted for risk and change.

Control principle: the five-year period is a maximum interval, not a default operational cycle. Earlier validation should be triggered by material risk, assessment changes, poor outcomes, complaints, trainer feedback or training-product change.

Smart & Skilled assurance

Funding & contract compliance for NSW Government-subsidised training, tracked separately from ASQA regulatory compliance — the two frameworks have different legal bases and shouldn't be blended, even though a gap in one can create risk in the other.

Two frameworks, tracked side by side: Regulatory compliance runs through ASQA / the 2025 Standards / Credential Policy (Obligations, Risk & Controls, Validation, Workforce, Standards Map). Funding & contract compliance runs through the NSW Smart and Skilled Operating Guidelines, Contract Terms and Conditions and Student Eligibility Policy — current for the 2026–27 activity period — tracked here. H&H's website identifies the RTO as a Smart and Skilled approved provider, which is why this demonstration includes a separate funding and contract assurance layer alongside ASQA regulatory assurance. Specific funded qualifications should be confirmed against current Smart and Skilled approval and Skills NSW information — H&H's own published pages aren't fully internally consistent on this (its course list and its fees/funding page use slightly different qualification codes for the same course), so this isn't a case where the site alone settles it.
